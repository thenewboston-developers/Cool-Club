How I came about thenewboston
Years ago, back when making video games was the only thing I wanted to do, I found thenewboston’s Java game development tutorials on YouTube. That series is where I wrote my first real game loop, my first sprite, my first collision check that actually worked — Bucky explaining all of it like it was the easiest thing in the world, one short video at a time.

Then I stumbled onto his “surviving the wilderness” vlog and watched the whole run. Coding tutorials one day, building a shelter out of sticks the next. That mix of “here is how a compiler works” and “here is how you start a fire in the rain” is a big part of why the channel stuck with me when plenty of others did not.

Fast forward a good number of years: I write software for a living now, the tutorials are back as XHTML, and somehow I am in the Cool Club. Full circle. Thanks, Bucky.



The Rabbits


The case for rabbits, exhibit A: they nap like this.

They are the reason my club title is Rabbit Enthusiast. In the interest of full disclosure, and for the membership committee’s consideration, here are some genuine rabbit facts:
  • Rabbits are not rodents. They are lagomorphs, in a club of their own with hares and pikas.
  • A happy rabbit does a binky: a mid-air jump and twist for no reason other than being pleased about something.
  • When a rabbit tips over onto its side out of nowhere, it has not fainted. That is a flop, and it means it feels safe.
  • Their teeth never stop growing, which is why they chew everything within reach, cables and skirting boards included.
  • Rabbits cannot vomit, so their gut has to keep moving in one direction. Unlimited hay is the whole job.
  • Each ear swivels almost 270 degrees on its own, and doubles as a radiator for dumping heat on a warm day.
  • They are crepuscular: busiest at dawn and dusk, flat out asleep through the middle of the day.
  • A rabbit has nearly 360-degree vision, with one blind spot: directly in front of its nose.
  • A content rabbit will softly grind its teeth. It is the bunny version of a cat’s purr.
